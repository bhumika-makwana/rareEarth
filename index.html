Thesis

Reprogramming resilience in living beings (biological memory), for life on Earth and beyond

Evolution is slow and systematic. It has been reprogramming resilience in living beings since centuries. Over time, it has gradually encoded biological memory which enables organisms today to adapt to rapid geological changes.

But, Planetary resilience is reaching its limits.

The environment today is completely re-engineered. Climate extremes, pollution, resource scarcity and emerging pathogens are altering ecosystems faster than the ability of these organisms to adapt.

And, Human biology faces a parallel problem.

Earth is the only planet we have co-evolved with the biosphere together over billions of years. As we go interplanetary, we will have to learn to optimize for what evolution never had to - Geotropism.

We will need to learn how to adapt biology deliberately.

The conditions once unique to space are beginning to emerge on Earth: resource scarcity, environmental extremes, pollution and increasingly hostile living conditions.

We're getting remarkably good at measuring and manipulating biology. By leveraging breakthroughs in genomics, robotics, and AI we explore potential pathways to accelerate the adaptation of species, enhance resilience, and expand the possibilities of life in a rapidly changing world.
